OPTIMIZER RESEARCH · DESIGN LAB · THREE EXHIBITS WITH LIVE DATA

The answer is a region.

Every “optimal portfolio” this dashboard reports is one point on a nearly flat valley floor of equally cheap plans. That is a measured fact about the physics, not a hedge — and it is the most useful thing the optimizer knows that today’s interface throws away. Three exhibits explore how to show it.

Data: 13 US zones, 8,760-hour chronological simulation, honest tax treatment (NOL carryforward + break-even LCOE), ~2.3M portfolio evaluations for these exhibits alone. Corpus & findings: optimizer_research/, July 2026. Lineage: modeling-to-generate-alternatives (DeCarolis 2011; Neumann & Brown 2021; Lombardi & Pfenninger 2025) — measured here by exhaustive enumeration rather than sampling.

EXHIBIT 01

One recommendation, honestly annotated

The design-debate verdict: keep a single recommended plan as the answer (people came for a plan), but make every slider whisper its freedom — the range it could take at essentially the same cost, and what it trades against. Bars show the ±1% cost band (darker) and ±2% (lighter); the tick is the recommendation.

EXHIBIT 03

The wall: what the last percent costs

Cost climbs gently for decades of percentage points, then hits a wall in the high nineties. The wall’s height is written into each zone’s single worst week of renewable drought — not into policy. Small multiples are ordered from tallest wall to shortest; the red segment is the road from 98% to 100%.

Abatement cost of the final step, 99% → 100%
Dollars per ton of CO₂ avoided. Reference: NREL’s system-wide estimate for the same step ($930/t) — islanded single zones with one firm technology pay 2–4× that.
The wall is drought physics
98→100% premium vs. the zone’s worst 168-hour renewable drought (deeper drought = shallower value). r = 0.78.

It’s a capacity problem, not an energy problem

The final percentage point eliminates nearly the same gas energy everywhere (~8,500–9,000 MWh) — yet its price varies almost 4× between zones. What differs is how much firm capacity must stand behind the worst week.

98% is a bargain; 100% is a decision

Across the fleet, going from 98% to 100% adds a mean +$16.9/MWh (+15%). Stopping at 98–99% keeps nearly all the carbon benefit at a fraction of the terminal cost — the same conclusion Princeton’s 24/7 work reached, now visible per zone.

Geography is destiny

Northwest’s wall (+9%) is a third of Southwest’s (+21%). No amount of optimization moves a zone between those worlds — only its weather, or transmission to a neighbor, can.
